## 1. Support Intake

### Channels
Client support requests may arrive through any agreed channel. All requests must be logged regardless of channel.

### Intake Steps
- [ ] Acknowledge receipt of the request within the response SLA
- [ ] Log the request with: client name, date/time, channel, description, urgency
- [ ] Categorise the request (see Section 2)
- [ ] Assign an owner
- [ ] Communicate expected resolution timeline to the client

---

## 2. Support Categories

| Category | Description | Example |
|----------|-------------|---------|
| C1 — General Inquiry | Questions about usage, features, or process | "How do I access X?" |
| C2 — Issue or Bug | Something is not working as expected | "The deliverable has an error" |
| C3 — Scope Change | Client requests changes beyond agreed scope | "Can we add Y to the deliverable?" |
| C4 — Escalation or Complaint | Client is dissatisfied and requests escalation | "I need to speak to someone senior" |
| C5 — Urgent or Critical | Time-sensitive issue with material business impact | "This is blocking our operations" |

## 3. Response SLAs

| Category | Acknowledgement | Resolution Target | Update Cadence |
|----------|----------------|-------------------|----------------|
| C1 | 4 business hours | 1 business day | At resolution |
| C2 | 2 business hours | 2 business days | Every 24 hours |
| C3 | 4 business hours | Per scope change process | Per scope change process |
| C4 | 1 business hour | 1 business day | Every 12 hours |
| C5 | 30 minutes | 4 business hours | Every 2 hours |

## 4. Resolution Workflow

### Stage 1: Triage
- Assess category and urgency
- Confirm the issue is within CCO scope
- If outside scope, route to correct department immediately and notify client

### Stage 2: Investigation
- Gather all relevant information
- Reproduce the issue if applicable
- Identify root cause or required action
- Determine if escalation is needed

### Stage 3: Resolution
- Execute the fix, answer, or action
- Communicate resolution to the client
- Confirm client satisfaction

### Stage 4: Closure
- Log resolution details
- Update client account record
- Capture any feedback or lessons learned
- Route product or technology implications to relevant departments

## 5. Escalation Matrix

| Level | Trigger | Owner | Action |
|-------|---------|-------|--------|
| L1 — Frontline | Standard C1/C2 request | CCO | Resolve directly per SOP |
| L2 — Senior | C2 unresolved after 24h, C3 scope change, C5 urgent | CCO | Investigate, engage Technology or Product as needed, communicate revised timeline |
| L3 — Executive | C4 complaint, L2 unresolved after 24h, material retention risk | CEO | CEO engages directly, cross-department resolution, client communication from CEO or CCO |

### Escalation Rules
- No issue may remain at any level beyond the escalation trigger without documented justification
- All escalations must be logged with: reason, timestamp, parties notified, action taken
- The client must be informed when their issue is escalated
- Escalation does not replace regular update cadence

## 6. Support Checklist

- [ ] Request received and acknowledged within SLA
- [ ] Request logged with all required details
- [ ] Category assigned
- [ ] Owner assigned
- [ ] Client informed of expected timeline
- [ ] Investigation completed
- [ ] Resolution executed or escalation initiated
- [ ] Client notified of resolution
- [ ] Client satisfaction confirmed
- [ ] Resolution logged in account record
- [ ] Feedback captured
- [ ] Product or technology implications routed if applicable

---

## 7. Support Definition of Done

A support request is not done unless:
- The issue is resolved or a clear path to resolution is communicated
- The client has been informed of the outcome
- The resolution is logged in the client account record
- Any escalation is fully documented
- Feedback is captured where relevant
- Product or technology implications are routed to the correct department
- No unresolved loose ends remain without documented owner and timeline
