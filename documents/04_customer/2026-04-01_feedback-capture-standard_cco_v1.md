## Feedback Sources

- Post-delivery surveys or check-ins
- Issue resolution follow-ups
- Scheduled check-in conversations
- Unsolicited customer communication
- Renewal conversations
- Onboarding completion feedback

## Feedback Capture Process

### 1. Capture
- Record feedback verbatim where possible
- Note source, date, customer, and context
- Do not filter, interpret, or soften feedback at capture stage

### 2. Categorize
- **Product feedback** — Feature requests, usability issues, product gaps
- **Delivery feedback** — Delivery quality, timing, communication, process
- **Support feedback** — Support experience, response quality, resolution
- **Commercial feedback** — Pricing, packaging, scope, contract terms
- **Strategic feedback** — Market positioning, competitive insights, expansion opportunities

### 3. Assess
- Determine urgency and impact
- Identify if feedback is isolated or systemic
- Flag any feedback that indicates churn risk or material dissatisfaction

### 4. Route
- **Product feedback** → Route to CPO with customer context and priority
- **Delivery feedback** → Handle internally, update delivery standards if needed
- **Support feedback** → Handle internally, update support SOP if needed
- **Commercial feedback** → Route to CSO with customer context
- **Strategic feedback** → Route to CEO with analysis

### 5. Close the Loop
- Inform customer that feedback was received and what action (if any) will be taken
- Track feedback item until resolution or acknowledgment by receiving team
- Do not promise specific product changes or timeline without CPO/CEO approval

## Definition of Done

Feedback handling is not done unless:
- Feedback is captured verbatim with full context
- Feedback is categorized correctly
- Feedback is routed to the correct team or handled internally
- Customer is informed of receipt and any action taken
- Feedback is logged in customer record
- Systemic patterns are flagged for review
